I think there's a sort of cultural faux pas in the RPG ecoculture that has evolved from a well-meaning social grace into an ossified, harmful truism, and I am going to challenge it, and insist that we reconsider it and its framing.

Let's see some examples, hm?

> Don’t hog the spotlight. Be aware of how much you talk, relative to the other players. If you’ve been talking a lot, suggest a spotlight change.  - Stonetop (Book 1, pg. 47)

> Seek consensus from the other players before barreling forward. Stay on the same page about goals and limits, respecting each other and accomplishing more as a group than alone.  - Cairn, Principles for Players

> During the action, spend the same amount of time making your character awesome and doing the same for others.  - ICRPG Power Tools Player Book, Pg. 1

> It’s not fun to watch someone sit and think. There are 3 or 4 other people at the table. They want to play. And time spent thinking is not time spent playing.  If you’re stuck for an idea, go with what you have. Or let someone else take the reins.  - Protocol Primer

All great points. I would never argue that this isn't good advice, almost universally true.

This advice helps the most when you have an imbalanced social dynamic at your table. Some people who are bold and outspoken and eager to jump in, others more reserved, waiting for the right moment, and more listening than speaking. Neither of these are better than the other, of course, but if you have friends in your group at the opposite ends of this spectrum, you have to consciously make the decision to share that spotlight (and, for the more reserved player, you also have to find opportunities to move into the spotlight. Sorry, it's not possible to play without playing!)
