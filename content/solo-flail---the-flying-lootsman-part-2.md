# Recap

In our last session, our guild of practically criminal, ethically challenged miscreants flew a rickety glider up to a flying pirate ship, crashed landed on the deck, murdered the invading pirates, attacked their captain, and were attacked by a giant Roc who is intent upon eating their bodies and regurgitating them to its fledglings.

All in a day's work.

# Let's go!

The roc, a massive bird of prey with a gnarled, twisted looking hooked beak, black eyes the size of pumpkins, and claws as long as broadswords and just as sharp, spreads its wings in its most intimidating posture. It's shriek of celebration in the cornering of its hapleess prey deafens the guild, sure to set their ears ringing for hours. From above in the crow's nest, eager, evil looking fledgling birds, each the size of a full grown adult human, look down on the upcoming carnage with anticipatory glee. If they had mouths, they'd be watering.

Gund shouts the warrior's battle cry and charges at the Roc. The bird moves with uncanny speed and thrusts its beak straight at the dwarf, who side steps the misshapen beak and brings the legendary blade Coral Whisper directly down on the bird's outstretched neck, severing it entirely in one blow.[^1] The head twitches a few times on the deck, the body flumps over the railing of the quarterdeck and lands on the main deck, it's lifeblood rapidly pumping out of the neck stump and coating the planks.

[^1]:@ Gund once again launches himself into the action, swinging his blade at the Roc and trying to mount it at the same time. On failure, the Roc gets a free attack with its beak.
d: TH6, 6d6=6,5,1,4,1,1
-> DEATH BLOW!
=> Gund launches himself straight at the Roc's beak, and with one clean swipe of his sword, beheads the Roc. (Holy shit!)

*GM Note: Death blows! One of the most exciting things about Flail is that anything, from a lowly centipede to a mighty dragon, can be brought down in a single blow... if you roll 3 1s on your To Hit roll. That's exactly what happened here, to the biggest and baddest threat on the ship. I **fucking love it.** What a moment! This is what TTRPGs are all about, the incredible moments that you will never forget from that time you did that incredible thing.*

The rest of the party stands motionless, shocked, impressed, and maybe a bit terrified of their warrior captain.

Gund turns back to the crew, panting heavily, a crazed bloodraged look in his eyes. "There's killing to be done. Get goin'."

Without another word, Gund turns and leaps down to the main deck past the slain corpse of the giant bird, lifts up the latticework hatch on the main deck, and jumps down. The rest of the crew gathers their wits and scrambles down the stairway and leaps down after their insane leader.[^2]

[^2]:@ The party has a few options here!
Obvious: Kill the kobold on the bowsprit who is blowing the warning horn with a terrified look on his face
Option: Scout out the hold of the ship
Odd: Jump straight into the hold and charge ahead.
d: 2d6 Doubling Down=6,6
-> Holy shit again! Doubled down on the odd. I'm marking "bloodraging" as one of Gund's traits, emphasizing that for the future, and we're gtting reckless.

As the rest of the party jumps into the hold, they are met with the sight of drawn blades and a hulking, battle scarred troll, ears pierced with a dozen golden rings, a heavy steel nose ring, tribal scarifications on its neck and chest, and, most frighteningly of all, carrying a heavy whip that crackles with electric energy.

Eight pirates are encircling Gund, moving sharklike around him as the rest of the crew lands. The pirates hesitate for a moment upon seeing reinforcement, a few glancing upwards through the hatch to see if any more are coming. But the sound of a whip crack refocuses their attention. "KILL THEM SLOW," shouts Grolg the Quartermaster, and the pirates leap at the chance to do some murdering.

Gund lashes out with Coral Whisper at the closest pirate, the seawater coated blade sending a spray of red blood across the timbers of the ship.

Behind him, Ember the kobold starts to glow, orange light suffusing orange scales, and her body transforms into a mighty brown bear. In her new apex predator form, she turns and roars, and rakes a meaty paw against the pirate that Gund struck, sending them flying into the hull of the ship with a sickening crunching sound.

The rest of the pirates, more terrified of the whip than the primal beast in front of them, start hacking and slashing with wild abandon. Nahar takes a blade in the shoulder, cutting deeply, and cries out in pain and collapses to the floor. Ember-Bear is set upon by four of the pirates and her own blood starts matting her brown fur. Gund intercepts a couple of pirates who set upon Glazbad, taking blades in his chest and helm intended for his half-orc compatriot.[^3]

*GM Notes: Combat is brutal. I had initially forgotten that you could mark armor to reduce the severity of a hit, so Gund originally went down as well as Nahar and I was certain this one-shot was coming to a miserable end. But that's why I'm doing this! I'm learning along the way. Once I found this out, I was less inclined to pull my punches as the GM, and to allow the full danger to set upon the group. I'm glad I did! Combat may be brutal, but it's so satisfying. Look at all those dice rolls!*

The troll quartermaster Grolg swings his brutal whip at Ember, which cracks harmlessly over her head. The electricity coming off of it makes her bear fur stand on end, making her even more terrifying in this cramped hold.

Glazbad pulls the blade out of Nahar's shoulder and presses his meaty half-orc hands on it to staunch the flow of blood. Under his breath, he chants a prayer to Zor'Vol, Lord of Chaos, his patron, to reinforce the life of his companion. In his head, he only hears a soft, rumbling chuckle, somehow full of anticipatory glee at the blood about to be shed. *Let the blood flow... Let it flow...*[^4]

Meanwhile, while Gund is carving into two of the pirates, Ember-Bear grins, the unnatural sight of a bear showing its toothy maw making one of the pirates in front of her cower in fear, and she lunges out and clamps her jaws down on the pirate's shoulder, crunching into bone and pulling the arm and shoulder clean off of the pirate's body. She horks down the appendage, the rusted shortsword that he was wielding clattering to the ground, and turns her bloody gaze on the rest as she devours him.

Several of the pirates immediately relieve themselves and tremble in front of this force of nature. Hesitation and fear plays across all of their eyes, but the sound of a whip crack just behind their heads snaps them out of their terrified reverie and back into the fight.[^5]

-> Success

The pirates set upon the guild again, driven to violence by the fear of the hulking quartermaster behind them. Blades fly, and the guild is pressed back. Ember growls in rage as blades score her bear face and legs. A blow lands on Gund's head, his helmet flying off and his head ringing. Glazbad is saved from impalement by his own chain armor but multiple blows leave him breathless and reeling.

The quartermaster laughs, an awful, bloodthirsty chortle, and the lightning whip snaps out again across Ember's snout, electricity leaving lightning-shaped burn scars across her face and head.[^6]

[^6]:@ Grolg the Quartermaster cracks his whip at Ember again.

*GM Note: Our first poker hand! It was a hit, and a 1,2,3,4,5 is a straight, which activates the Lightning Whip's special feature, which is an entire D8 of extra damage on the hit. Huge. Also very fun! Players will be rolling a bunch of dice and looking for these poker hands, which can give effects ranging from... well, I won't spoil it here. Go get FLAIL! It's epic!*

Glazbad, still locked into communion with his demonic patron, removes his hand from Nahar's wound and turns to face the pirates with fire in his eyes. From his mouth come words not in his own voice, but of some hellish fiend, something that was never meant to be uttered in this world. "Zor'Vol, Lord of Chaos, Devour These Pirates!"

The wooden planks of the floor underneath the pirates seem to warp and blur, twist into a different reality, as an abscess opens in the world, a cavern of Hell itself, and swallows the pirates into infinite oblivion. Gund barely grabs. Nahar's leg before the pale human slides into the maw as well, and pulls him back away from the pit.[^7]

[^7]:A great mechanic here: praying for divine intervention! Once per session, pray for anything. It happens, but you have to roll a d6 for each word of your prayer, and it comes at _great risk_. Also, GMs should really consider monkey's paw-ing this type of thing. In this case, the maw was fully capable of swallowing their own guild mates as well. Zor'Vol doesn't discriminate!

Now alone, the troll's face for the first time shows something other than confidence. Gund takes advantage of the momentary hesitation and Coral Whisper draws troll-blood. Ember-Bear claws at the quartermaster as well, and claw marks rake across its chest. As she strikes the troll, her body starts to shift back, and she is left kneeling on the deck in her kobold form.

Grolg, now fully outmatched and outmaneuvered, falls backwards onto his ass, and scrambles backwards like a crab, outright terror evident on his scarred face. "No! I surrender! Parlay?? Parlay! You let me surrender!" The lightning whip is tossed across the floor, sliding to Ember's feet. The kobold woman, panting from primal exertion, leans down and carefully grabs the leather wrapped handle.

Gund puts the point of his blade in front of Grolg's face. "How do I fly this bathtub of a vessel?"

Grolg's eyes flicker between Gund and the gaping maw of hell behind him. "Air spirit... in the fo'c'sle[^8]... Skarx shove it in a machine, makes the ship fly... He have the key to the fo'c'sle..."

[^8]: Forecastle!

Glazbad, who was knelt by Nahar to tend his wounds, channels energy into the necromancer. As he does, the fire fades from his eyes and the portal to hell closes, leaving disturbingly undamaged planks of wood in its place. He looks up at Gund with his normal eyes. "Let's get the key and get back on the ground. Nahar needs better tending to than I can give him."

Gund nods, wearied by the evaporation of the blood rage that had gripped him. He sheaths his blade. "Leave the troll here. He's in no shape to do any more fighting."

The guild climbs out of the hold of the ship and heads to the captain's quarters. The door is open, and the captain is nowhere to be seen. The desk that he had been manacled to lies askew, one of the legs broken out, and the manacles themselves are gone as well.[^9]

Gund's stomach drops. "Oh, hells..." He races out of the room to the fore of the ship, and sees the forecastle, and the kobold captain limping through an open door of iron bars. He launches himself off the quarterdeck rails, stumbling over the corpse of the Roc.

Skarx starts pulling at machinery, breaking pipes and chains off of the ship's wind engine. A long, loud shriek of rushing air mutes Gund's desperate shouts for the captain to stop. In the air over the deck, an elemental coalesces into being, a living whirlwind, a tornadic sphere. The captain screams in jubilation and leaps off of the front of the ship, spreading his arms and legs to reveal that he's wearing a wingsuit, and he flies off into the distance as the ship tilts forward dramatically and begins to plummet into the city below.[^10]

The elemental buffets the guild with wind as it begins to ascend higher away from the ship. Ember, still worn ragged, and with a fading echo of the lightning burn scar on her face, stumbles forward and lifts her face to the elemental. Her eyes glow with druidic energy as she speaks in a language that none present have ever spoken, nor will ever understand.

"Great spirit, do not abandon us to this fate! Without your aid we will surely die. We have fought your captor! We have indirectly won your freedom! Will you deliver us from death in return?"

The spirit pauses in its ascent. Though it has no face and no eyes, Ember can still feel its supernatural gaze, and can barely keep from wilting under its force of authority.

"Captor... I will find them and rend them in my storm..." The words are the sound of a god shouting into a hurricane. To all but Ember, the shape of the sound is unrecognizable, unthinkable, something to turn their mind away from.

"We can... we can aid you. We can find the one that bound you! Lend us your breath and we will give you your vengeance!"[^11]

The elemental pauses for a long moment, then descends to the ship, and re-enters the engine. Ember, ever the curious mechanical mind, puts back into place the pieces broken by Skarx, and just before the ship crashes into the city below, rights the vessel skyward. Gund claps the kobold druid on the back with a heavy, bloody hand. "Well done, girl. Now get us off this awful contraption. I've done enough flying for a year or three."

Thus, the party, with a powerful elemental of air voluntarily bound in an arcane bottle, set out on a quest to find the pirate Skarx, to fulfill their obligation to the elemental to whom they owe their lives.

# Conclusion

Whew! What a ride! Over 5000 words of solo play. That's a lot, for me especially. But between Flail, Triple-O, and the lonelog notation system, I was able to stay in the flow of it and see the entire thing through to completion.

I'm really into this whole setup. Flail might not be my core solo campaign game, but it's amazing for shorter campaigns and one-shots. I can already tell it's got a lovely place in my rotation. Sword and sorcery vibes, hack and slash, pulpy fiction - it's not the only thing I want in my campaigns, but it's definitely something I want to include.

Soloing as the GM using Triple-O is, of course, a pleasure. Go check it out. It's an invaluable part of my prep and toolset now.

Until next time!
