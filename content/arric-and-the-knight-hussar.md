A mirage against the lavender sky flickers and flits. It draws closer and makes itself known to Arric some hundreds of meters on; a flying craft arrayed in the shape of a clover, four gossamer membranes around a central cradle. The salt of the plains vibrates like dust on a drumhead as the Knight Hussar's craft alights fifty paces from Arric's campfire.

Arric stands, brushing salt and sand from his legs and eyes the Knight Hussar. Goggles decorated in the usual style, with lenses in an avian shape and a leather band adorned with feathers, hide the warrior's expression. When the knight leaves their long barreled rifle in the egg shaped pod, Arric lays his own weapon down and approaches with his palms outstretched.

"Have I given offense or trespassed on lands under your protection, Master Hussar?" Arric calls out across the gap. Wind blows steadily from the south but not so hard that his voice can't carry, so he makes his way toward the flyer at a leisurely pace. "If so, please accept my humble apologies and a promise to depart at once."

The Knight Hussar pulls his goggles back from his eyes and his sky blue scarf down from his mouth, revealing a thick black moustache and wrinkled eyes, wrinkles that deepen as he grins widely. His armor, of the same pure sky blue color as the scarf, reflects the setting red sun and the white salt of the plains.  He laughs, rich, syrupy and genuine, and Arric feels the tension fall away from his shoulders. 

"No, no, nothing of the sort! I just tired of the cold air and thought I might impose upon the hospitality of one of the plains, to sit and warm myself at your fire."

Arric gestures back toward the fire. "My light and water are yours, honored one." The men exchange a modest sip of water from each other's skins, and with that the ceremony and pretense yield to the comforts of camp.

The Knight sighs heavily, and what before seemed like a face wrinkled by endless joy becomes one wrinkled by heavy burden. "I've been flying for two days, and I'm not a young man anymore. In my day I could circle the world in my glider, just to land on the battlefield and leap into the fight. Now, my legs are numb and my ass still vibrates." He snorts with a wry grin. "Appreciate the braiding of your life, young man. One day you will only complain about your aches and pains."

Arric sits next to the fire and offers the older man his pouch of dried meat. Arric's dramak, unsaddled and unladen, stands on its many gangly legs and stalks away to find more cattail reeds, unbothered by the arrival of the knight and his aircraft. The old man glances at Arric with a raised eyebrow.

"Oh, she won't go far. She's an old coward. If the there's so much as a strong gust, she'll scuttle back." Arric smiles fondly at his mount. "Why do you fly today? For pleasure or business?"

The old man shakes his head with a soft smile, and does not answer.

Arric nods and lays back on the reedy salt sands, hands crossed behind his head. "I have dreamed of flying, long as I can remember. When I was a boy, the old Imperium would patrol the villages in the south and the west. They would set down in their ornithopters and all the children would come running to see them, both machine and their renowned pilots. We would prod the younger children to run up and touch one, but the pilots were strict and not shy with their polearms. Always the blunt end, obviously. But even the blunt end could crack your skull, so for the most part we were behaved."

"It is a wondrous thing, up there. To see the whole world from above, to see the plains racing by. I've loved it all my life. I knew I would be a Knight Hussar long before manhood." The old man rips a piece of jerky off with his teeth and softens it with a bit of water. "The salt plains are your home then?"

"All my life. Born in the Gesta flower fields during the blooming season. My mother said that meant I was blessed, and would be fortunate all my life. I am still here, still breathing, still riding, so perhaps she was right." 

"I have seen the Gesta flowers in bloom! It is the greatest beauty on the plains. I spent a summer and autumn with a girl, in my youth, and we followed the flowers across the salt pan. This was before I took my oaths." The knight smiles fondly, tossing dead, dry reed onto the fire. "I think she married and left for the city, maybe Caibo? I can't remember."

Arric feels his heart sink for the old man. Caibo was razed near the beginning of the war that ended the Imperium. People only told stories of that war as warning - against trusting outsiders, against leaving the village for the city, against fealty to lords at all. The two sit in quiet for a few minutes. Arric does not break the silence. The dramak comes back, curls her legs up under her body, and lays her insectoid head against the ground. This shakes the two men out of their reverie, and the Knight Hussar stands up and brushes himself off.

"Ah, I suppose I should move on. The stories of your peoples' hospitality are true, my friend on the plains. If you find yourself in need, you can call upon old Sharrukenazzar at the Syfort, where I am most often found."

Arric stands, takes off his hat and shakes the old man's hand. "Sharrukenazzar, fair winds for you." As the Knight Hussar clambers up into his aircraft's cradle, he salutes Arric in the Hussar manner, closed fist raised over his head. The vehicle's sheer, nearly invisible membranes begin to vibrate louder and louder until it kicks off, disappearing against the lavender sky.
