[Dungeon patch!](https://riseupcomus.blogspot.com/2026/09/the-elemental-level-of-megadungeon.html) It's the new thing. Basically, make a 5-6 room bit of dungeon, with strong themes and ideas, and stitch it together with other patches into a quilt, and suddenly you have a nice big dungeon!

[Rise Up Comus](https://riseupcomus.blogspot.com/2026/09/making-dungeon-patch-sausage-making.html) made a stream of consciousness blog article where he ideated and sketched out a dungeon patch that he's going to tidy up and publish at some point (hopefully!) That emboldens me to do the same, so here we are. I will take an idea, talk about it for a while, show you my thought process, and at the end hopefully we'll have a 5ish room dungeon patch that you can happily steal.

The theme of my dungeon seed that this patch is going into is - a lighthouse on an underground lake that draws ships from the "overworld" down into this "underworld" and shatters them amongst the rocks, providing bodies and treasure for the necromancer who lives within. A river feeds the lake, and every so often a ship will materialize and rampage down the river, breaking apart before the crew meets their demise in the lake.

My patch is going to involve the froglins who live in this underground cavern system and who dredge the river for treasures. Froglins have been crafted up on the [His Majesty the Worm](https://discord.gg/D97e8dRyF9) discord and are goblin analogs, but aquatic and different enough to avoid the vanilla fantasy problem.

![Froglins!](/images/Pasted image 20260907091957.png)

So these froglins, led by their chief Kaz'kilek, are dredging the river for magical treasure. Why? Their chief, Kaz'kilek, found a magical conch shell here when they arrived, and it whispers secrets to her when she holds it to her ear. Ever since then, she's been obsessed with finding more trinkets. She and her tribe decided to settle here - maybe they are searching for enough magical goods to go back to their home? I have the vague idea that something (maybe The Wyrm?) chased them out of their ancestral home. They could be stockpiling artifacts to use to take it back.

I read a [good blog post](https://guccifuligincloak.blogspot.com/2020/01/the-four-blanks-method-of-faction.html) a long time ago about generating factions. It said they need four things:

1. The faction. (this is the froglins, obviously.)
2. What they want
3. Why they can't have it
4. What they are doing to get around the problem

In this case, they want magical treasures to get enough power to go home. They can't have it because the caves are patrolled by undead sailors led by their admiral with strict orders to keep the caverns secure for the Necromancer. What are they doing about that?

![Undead Sailors](/images/Pasted image 20260907092642.png)

I imagine they have some sort of barricaded den. A safe place to sleep and all that. Other than that, rather than just going out and driving off the undead, I think they sneak around a lot, climbing on the walls and ceilings and dropping down behind the undead, Mission Impossible style, to grab some junk and detritus and hop back to their den to sort it out.

So they have secret passages and they grab as much as their little webbed hands can hold and head home. What do they do with it when they are there?

They must sort it out into nice stuff and crap, and they've gotta get rid of the crap somewhere. I have an elemental theme going on in this dungeon seed elsewhere, so let's put a fire elemental incinerator down here. It needs to be interesting though. The incinerator could get backed up if the froglins are tossing trash down the chute and if it gets clogged or backed up, the pressure could make it all explode. It's happened before, so now the froglins have some "chimney sweeps" making sure things don't get backed up. If the players interfere and don't know about the chimney, they could be in for trouble...

## Rooms

Let's list the rooms we obviously have.

1. Barricaded Den
2. Sorting Room
3. Incinerator
4. The riverside
5. Empty Room

I mean, that's a dungeon patch if I ever saw one. 4-5 rooms. I need to review the checklist from Rise Up Comus:

> 1. Something that advances your [dungeon checklist](https://dungeons.hismajestytheworm.games/docs/chapter5/)
> 2. 3-6 keyed rooms
> 3. An intentionally blank room that can hold a [key for a lock](https://riseupcomus.blogspot.com/2024/01/metroidvanias-and-megadungeons.html) in another section of the Underworld. I should call these spaces **potential connections.**
> 4. A **gate** that connects to a different section of the Underworld.
> 5. An evocative **name**

I think we can safely say the dungeon checklist is advanced in a couple of ways here. We have 5 rooms that we can key. We have an intentionally blank room that can hold something for whatever GM is using this material.

We need a gate and we need a name and we need to key the rooms! I had a conversation on discord about the gate thing. Having a gate to a different dungeon level in every patch seems like overkill to me, so I'd probably only write about half my patches to have a gate - but since this is my first patch, I'll stick to the exercise.

I think the sorting room could be an interesting place to put it. Below a huge pile of unsorted trash, a trapdoor to another level? Could be a fun rewards for helping the froglins sort their backlog. A neat little surprise. The fire elemental could be hiding a gateway to another level as well. I like the idea of roleplaying with this guy - he wants to go back to his elemental plane, but needs to be carried somewhere else in the megadungeon (safely!) to do so. So if you can safely brave the trash chute and the incinerator, you could find another dungeon level down there as well. I'll decide later.

As far as the name - The Froglin Trash Warren? It's as good as any. Maybe you have a better idea for me. Okay, let's flesh out the rooms a little more in depth.

1. **Barricaded Den**. A haphazard, patchwork wall made of shipwreck planks and tied together with rigging cuts the room in half. A couple murder holes and a closed door in the center. Outside of the wall, random elbow bones and foot bones scattered the floor, left behind by repelled undead. Hushed voices and quiet shuffling on the other side. Froglins are behind the wall, and this is where they draw the line against the undead sailors, poking spears and firing bows and slings at them until they are driven away. Inside, a few dozen froglins bunk up in a crazed array of hammocks made of cut sailcloth. It smells like fish and dung. [Monsters, Social]
2. **The Sorting Room.** Five floor to ceiling piles of random junk. (Spend a turn searching to try to find common adventuring gear - it'll collapse in an avalanche of trash on a critical failure.) Froglins unceremoniously toss river detritus onto the piles, while other froglins dig through the piles looking for things shiny, unusual, colorful. Underneath one of the piles is a rusted iron grate that leads to another level of the dungeon. [Treasure, Monsters]
3. **Incinerator.** Down a cluttered chute coated with soot and ash, regularly swept by froglins lest the chamber below build up pressure and explode. Inside is a brass furnace, holding a fire elemental named Kthrxx-Sshhh-Ffrrrk, who happily burns everything shoveled into his chamber but hopes one day to be carried out of this place and to the Molten Gate in the Basalt Crags (in another dungeon level). If the froglins are disturbed and don't sweep the chute, add an explosion timer to the Meatgrinder. [Monster, Social, Trap]
4. **Riverside**. Hanging from a rock wall are a bunch of makeshift fishing nets and a broken wooden bridge. The other half of the wooden bridge dangles from an opening on the other side of the river. The froglins use the nets to dredge the river. Several froglins lurk in the water and on the ceiling, waiting in the darkness to ambush undead who patrol this way - but they aren't particularly good at discerning between undead and adventurers (and both have treasures anyway.) An abandoned campfire from another adventuring party. [Monster, Ambush]
5. **Empty**. A pool of water here is slick with muck, but the contamination has no obvious source. Crude carvings in the wall from other adventurers warn of dangers ahead, but they look like they were carved by an idiot using their feet instead of their hands. Hard to decipher. [Empty]

That's good enough for me for now. I would key this for His Majesty the Worm more fluently were I to publish this, but the idea is pretty much there. I might add a few more details or spice up a few possibilities, but each of these rooms feels like they have interactivity and points of connection.

I could really get used to building like this, and I might. In fact, I was considering what a Dungeon Patches 2027 might look like. For one week, you spend 1 day ideating and coming up with the theme, days 2-6 are spent keying up five different rooms, one per day, and on day 7 you draw the map and connect your rooms. After a year of doing a patch per week, you'd have a 250 room megadungeon. That ain't bad, and it feels much more manageable than some of the other dungeon-a-day approaches I've seen.

Alright, au revoir. Maybe you'll see me in a week with another patch.
