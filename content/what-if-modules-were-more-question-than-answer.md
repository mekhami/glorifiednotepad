Questions as adventure content
Anti-canon
Infinite reflections of an interesting core idea
What if? questions, leading questions, Stonetoppy questions
Would you use this? Would you pay for something like this?

I love reading adventures. A great one sets my brain on fire. It sends me to somewhere totally novel, gives me just enough to be curious and eager, and my mind races with the fractal possibilities offered up by players as unfolding narratives.

Some of them have canonical settings, some are jewelboxes set in blank spaces. Some are modular regions that you could find a place for anywhere. They all get drawn in somewhere. And with my recent obsession with everything Stonetop and everything questions, I started to ask - What would it look like to read an adventure that poses those questions to me as the GM, and allowed me to color within the lines?

Anti-canon settings spark a similar joy in me. Some of them are just excuses to say "you can add more stuff to this however you like", but truly great anti-canon settings give you something great to work with and seed you with ideas and tools to build those ideas out. What if those tools were questions?

The leading question style of Stonetop and other games in the genre could be a perfect fit here. I think about the outcome that I want and work backwards from there.

I want, at the end of the process, a setting of some size that feels mine but started with enough structure (in the form of leading questions) that I was always not just beyond the blank page, but well on my way to something impressive. I want the questions to be aimed at me as a worldbuilder - not me as the GM, not the players, not the party, but at the person that just wants to inhabit this new world.
