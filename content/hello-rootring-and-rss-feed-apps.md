# Hello, Rootring!

You may have noticed that little widget to the bottom left of the page. If you haven't, what are you doing? Well, that's the Rootring, a modern Webring for TTRPG blogs. Brought to you by the incredible [elmcat](https://elmc.at), if you check click next or prev on the Rootring widget, you'll get taken to another random awesome TTRPG blog that you can explore. And they'll have a link to another, so on and so forth. Webrings!

Webrings have a long history. They were popular in the 90s, they got sort of co-opted by Yahoo, who failed to monetize the idea, so they basically killed all the webrings. Figures. Corporate soul sucking profit seeking bullshit. Isn't it exhausting?

Webrings are one of many ways we can take the internet back from these soulless megacorporations. We don't need facebook, instagram, or tiktok to discover new things that interest us. We don't need profit driven algorithms stoking anger and hatred, driving us into depression. We can just... join like-minded people and share what we're doing. We can own our own websites and link them together in a better version of the internet, the one we had in the 90s that was a revolution in creative expression. It's not even hard.

You may have seen my [last post on Substack](https://mekhami.substack.com/p/the-indie-web-the-rpg-blogosphere). It'll go more into depth on why this site exists, why that one is ended, and what this webring will mean for the RPG Blogosphere.

I believe strongly in this. I encourage you to participate - whether it's adding your own writing to the Rootring, or by bookmarking it and checking in from time to time. Let's seize the internet from the oligarchs.

# RSS Feed Apps

Which brings me to another point. It can be hard to curate things you're interested in. Some blogs that I like won't be on the rootring (although I think they should be!) Some feeds aren't blogs at all, and I want to create useful lists of those. So far, I've used a pretty underwhelming chrome extension called Feed Fabrik RSS Feed Reader. It's glitchy and unintuitive, and it's not cross platform.

So I'm asking you for advice - do you know of good, cross platform RSS apps? If not cross platform, perhaps applications that can export their list of feeds and import into other applications? Surely something exists, but I have a hell of a time trying to find one. Please, if you know something, leave a comment. This is another important part of taking the internet back.

Thanks for reading, thanks for being here. I'm excited for the future. I'll get back to writing about TTRPGs soon, when I have a little more energy than I have right now.
