In Science Fantasy, one of my favorite tropes is "deep history" or "deep time". I.e., time scales of civilizations that stretch beyond understanding. Hundreds of empires rising and falling. The world changes drastically from one eon to the next, to accommodate its dominant lifeforms and eventually the world changes without their consent and ends that collective civilization. Rinse and repeat as desired.

These worlds are series of worlds. Each one can and often does look like an entirely different world. A different makeup of dominant biologies, a different path of socio-economic patterns, a different general zeitgeist and collective cultural thinking. Much like all of the different worlds built to serve the TTRPG hobby. If you took all of the worlds you could think of and strung them together in a row, you'd get a pretty interesting view of how 100 billion years looks to a world.

Vaarn certainly feels this way to me, and I am sure it owes some amount of that inspiration to Caves of Qud, which is certainly my favorite example of deep history. Societies that could build a space elevator and create a coven of interstellar civilizations to join into one universal community... those same societies fallen, their statues crumbled, their space elevator the only standing monument to their achievement, and it changed, in disrepair. When you play with time on this scale, every genre and time period could be spliced in. At some point, those space elevator people also had their analogue to office buildings and combustion engine cars and internets and demagogues. But all we have left to witness is their greatest spectacle, and that is how they are known to this world.

I'd imagine if you started shoving random worlds together and working out how they might connect, what their own time scales are, you would end up synthesizing some novel ideas. And maybe you'd want to zoom in on what happened in between, and now you traverse the world fractally, expanding out and in at your leisure like higher dimensional beings walking across time.

If this is waxing a little poetic, it's because I get a little romantic. About deep time and cosmic scales, and the way fantasy and science fiction fit together, about how that gradient of crossfading between them is so interesting to explore. Give me more Orn. Give me Gene Wolfe (but hold the sexism, please.) Give me Path of Exile's world of Wraeclast. I'll find my way to EVE Online's New Eden Cluster eventually. And then we can all meet back together again in Brandonsford.
